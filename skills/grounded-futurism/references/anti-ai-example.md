# Worked example: an anti-AI political upheaval

Read when assessing anti-AI political scenarios or their labor-market premises.
These mechanisms are hypotheses to investigate, not predictions or a required sequence.

## Define the outcome before choosing a route

Opposition, restrictions, violent resistance, durable domestic prohibition, and global economic disruption have different prerequisites.
A prohibition can pass without effective enforcement; economic destruction can spread without other countries adopting it.
Destructive consequences need not wait for a movement to win power or achieve its aims.

An economic route might connect deployment, reduced human labor demand, unresolved hardship, political organization, and prohibition.
Each connection needs support.
Reinstatement failure—new human work not compensating for displacement—may accelerate this route without being necessary for every route.
Safety incidents, moral or religious mobilization, national-security policy, and regime change offer other hypotheses.
A movement can blame AI for unrelated hardship: accurate attribution is unnecessary for scapegoating, but a compelling story does not explain organization or victory.
For imposing demands, identify who coordinates supporters and how they acquire the means.

Choose prerequisites for the particular route.
Opponents could reject compensation and technology on principle, without failed redistribution.
A governing elite could impose prohibition without mass mobilization.
These possibilities establish questions, not likelihoods.

Compare an organized campaign imposing a ban with fragmented attacks inducing service withdrawal, retaliation, and lost maintenance capacity.
The latter could produce widespread destruction without unified intent or political victory.
Selective targeting need not have selective consequences.
Trace how disruption spreads, whether repair and substitution keep pace, and whether coordination forms during conflict.
Durable enforcement may require capacity that disruption does not; repair, substitution, or de-escalation could interrupt either route.

When reinstatement failure is stipulated, assess political responses within it rather than mainly questioning displacement.
If welfare and distributed ownership already occupy branches in the supplied model, locate them there.
They can compete with destruction but do not by themselves refute the branch where accommodation fails.

## Follow a response through failure and correction

Dependence can raise rejection's cost while increasing resentment, coercive leverage, and damage from attacks or withdrawal.
Test whether repression deters, strengthens, or divides resistance across groups; radicalization need not improve the capacity to impose prohibition.
Trace international harm through production, service, financial, or supply-chain dependencies, checking redundancy, substitution, and recovery.
An international abolitionist movement is unnecessary for spillovers; integration alone does not establish catastrophe.

Selective restrictions or infrastructure seizure need their own consequence analysis.
For example, reserving occupations for humans could raise costs relative to automated foreign competitors.
Would sustaining the policy require trade restrictions, remove needed inputs, or disrupt others dependent on the output?
A takeover could preserve production if operators, inputs, and service relationships survive; intact hardware alone does not establish continuity.

Now follow disappointing results.
Shortages could discredit the program and prompt retreat, or strengthen sabotage accusations and expand repression's targets.
Test a loop in which failed remedies become evidence of sabotage, purges reduce recovery capacity, and further failures justify broader purges.
Compare policy reversal, tolerated failure, selective exemptions, and ineffective campaigns.
Look for organizations, narratives, and state responses that favor one route.
Persecution can become a consequence without being an initial aim; hardship and an escalating example establish neither its occurrence nor its likelihood.

## Discover actors through the mechanism

Inspect factions, organizers, and recruitment networks alongside parties and governments.
Published policy establishes stated commitments, not everyone who could acquire power.
Current support for AI weighs against straightforward continuation into prohibition, not necessarily replacement by an opposing coalition.

Compare actors who possess means of persecution with those who could acquire them during crisis.
For opposition movements, examine elections, governing-coalition influence, or institutional fragmentation where relevant.
Test mechanisms with demonstrated behavior, not regime labels.
Ideological destruction elsewhere does not establish a connection to AI grievances; current AI support does not establish responses to failed employment promises.
A model of weakened workplace bargaining does not establish political stability if it excludes protest or state breakdown.

## Test the labor premise separately

Distinguish AI capability and activity from economically equivalent replacement of paid work.
Check whether measured deployment changes hiring, employment, paid hours, wages, or human labor per unit of quality-adjusted output.
Lower labor per unit need not reduce total demand if output expands; employment growth at adopters need not be causal if growing firms select into adoption.

When AI-specific causal evidence is required, prioritize credible adoption-linked designs with appropriate controls.
Distinguish them from replaced-workflow descriptions, exposure correlations, announcements, benchmarks, and aggregate trends.
Inspect selection, offshoring, outsourcing, restructuring, and the business cycle as competing explanations.
Track whether new tasks create paid work that survives later deployment, not just new titles.
Preserve governing evidence hierarchies and notification thresholds; scenario plausibility does not satisfy them.

## Choose evidence that separates routes

For the economic route, check attributable labor-demand changes and offsetting new work.
For mobilization, inspect organization, demands, coalition durability, and access to institutional or coercive power.
For cumulative disruption, check whether failures spread across otherwise separate incidents and whether recovery capacity declines or adapts.
For international disruption, inspect dependencies and alternatives, not how many countries share an ideology.
State what would weaken each route as well as strengthen it.
