# Worked example: an anti-AI political upheaval

Read when assessing anti-AI political scenarios or their labor-market premises.
These are candidate mechanisms to investigate, not established predictions or a required sequence.

## Separate the outcomes

Distinguish opposition, restrictions, violent resistance, durable domestic prohibition, and global economic disruption.
One can occur without the others.
A prohibition can pass without effective enforcement; economic destruction can spread without other countries adopting the prohibition.
Do not silently equate a movement's goals with its consequences.
Destructive consequences need not wait for a movement to win power or successfully impose prohibition.

## Construct alternative routes

An economic route might connect deployment, reduced human labor demand, unresolved hardship, political organization, and prohibition.
Each connection needs separate support.
Reinstatement failure—new human work not compensating for displacement—is one possible accelerant, not a prerequisite for every route.

Other candidate routes include a safety incident, moral or religious mobilization, national-security policy, or regime change.
A movement can also blame AI for hardship caused by unrelated events.
Accurate causal attribution is unnecessary for scapegoating, but a compelling story alone does not explain organization or victory.
For a route requiring the imposition of demands, identify who coordinates supporters and how they acquire the means to do so.

Do not make failed redistribution a prerequisite: opponents could reject both compensation and the technology on principle.
Do not make mass mobilization a prerequisite for state prohibition: a governing elite could impose it for its own reasons.
These alternatives establish questions to investigate, not evidence that any route is likely.

Compare an organized campaign imposing a technology ban with fragmented attacks that induce service withdrawal, retaliation,
and loss of maintenance capacity.
The second route may cause widespread destruction without a unified movement or achievement of the first route's political objective.
Selective targeting does not establish selective consequences.
Identify how disruption spreads, whether repair and substitution keep pace, and whether coordination develops during the conflict.
Sustained enforcement of a prohibition may need capacity that disruption does not.
Investigate whether repair, substitution, and de-escalation interrupt the process;
the existence of an escalation mechanism alone does not establish its likelihood.

When reinstatement failure is stipulated, do not answer mainly by questioning whether displacement will occur.
Assess the political responses within that condition.
If the supplied model already includes welfare and distributed ownership, locate those responses in its existing branches.
They can compete with a destructive outcome, but do not by themselves refute the branch where accommodation fails.

## Trace interactions without fixing their direction in advance

Dependence can increase the cost of rejection while increasing resentment, coercive leverage, and disruption from attacks or withdrawal.
Examine whether repression deters resistance, strengthens it, divides it, or produces different responses among different groups.
Do not assume radicalization makes a movement more capable of achieving prohibition.

Trace international consequences through specific production, service, financial, or supply-chain dependencies.
Identify redundancy, substitution, and recovery capacity alongside transmission mechanisms.
Domestic upheaval need not become an international abolitionist movement to cause harm abroad.
Neither dependence nor international integration alone establishes catastrophic consequences.

Do not assume selective restrictions or seizure of infrastructure are non-destructive alternatives.
For example, reserving occupations for humans could increase production costs relative to automated foreign competitors.
Investigate whether sustaining the policy then requires trade restrictions, what inputs become unavailable, and who else depends on the affected output.
A takeover could preserve production if operators, inputs, and service relationships survive; intact hardware alone does not establish continuity.

Trace whether resulting economic breakdown changes the movement itself.
Shortages could discredit its program and prompt retreat, or strengthen accusations of sabotage and expand the targets of repression.
Investigate the organizations, narratives, and state responses that distinguish these routes.
For example, test a loop in which failed remedies become evidence of sabotage, purges reduce recovery capacity, and further failures justify broader purges.
Compare it with policy reversal, tolerated failure, selective exemptions, and ineffective campaigns;
an escalating example does not establish which response prevails.
Persecution need not be an initial aim to become a consequence, but hardship alone does not establish that it will occur.

## Compare governments and opposition movements

Inspect factions, movement organizers, and recruitment networks alongside parties and governments.
Published policy establishes stated commitments; it does not establish the full set of actors who could acquire power.
Current support for AI weighs against straightforward continuation into prohibition;
it does not by itself weigh against replacement by an opposing coalition.

Compare who has the means to carry out persecution with who could acquire them during the crisis.
For opposition movements, distinguish access through elections, governing-coalition influence, or institutional fragmentation where these routes matter.
Use demonstrated behavior to test a mechanism, not regime labels as substitutes for evidence.
A record of ideological destruction does not establish a connection to AI grievances;
current support for AI does not establish how an actor responds when its employment promises fail.
A model of weakened workplace bargaining does not establish political stability if it excludes protest or state breakdown.

## Test the labor premise separately

Distinguish AI capability and activity from economically equivalent replacement of paid human work.
Ask whether measured deployment changes hiring, employment, paid hours, wages, or human labor per unit of quality-adjusted output.
Reduced labor per unit does not establish reduced total labor demand if output expands.
Employment growth at adopters does not establish a positive causal effect if growing firms select into adoption.

Where the request requires AI-specific causal evidence, prioritize credible adoption-linked designs with appropriate controls.
Separate those from direct descriptions of replaced workflows and from exposure correlations, announcements, benchmarks, or aggregate trends.
Inspect selection, offshoring, outsourcing, restructuring, and the business cycle as competing explanations.
Track whether new human tasks create paid work and survive later deployment, not merely whether new titles or tasks appear.
Preserve any governing evidence hierarchy or notification threshold; scenario plausibility does not satisfy it.

## Choose observations that distinguish routes

For an economic route, look for attributable changes in labor demand and whether new work offsets them.
For a mobilization route, examine organization, demands, coalition durability, and access to institutional or coercive power.
For cumulative disruption, examine whether failures spread across otherwise separate incidents and whether recovery capacity is declining or adapting.
For international disruption, examine the affected dependencies and alternatives rather than counting countries sharing an ideology.
State what observation would weaken each route as well as what would strengthen it.
