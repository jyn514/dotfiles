# Whole-artifact editorial pass

Use the artifact, reader, purpose, evidence, and authoring or review-only scope established by the caller.
If these are missing, establish them through [holistic-writing](../SKILL.md).
Reuse settled decisions; this pass does not restart their workflows or authorize changing their substance.
A review request means findings, not edits.
Keep the destination unless relocation is requested.
If a needed specialized review is unavailable, say so rather than claiming this pass supplies it.

## 1. Read as the intended reader

Read the complete artifact, including dull stretches, before diagnosing its craft.
What comes to matter, what do you expect next, and what stays with you afterward?
Distinguish the experience of following the prose from what it makes you understand or feel about its subject.
A story can be enjoyable to read and leave danger felt; technical detail can make an ordinary object worth noticing.
Naming a technique or reconstructing the facts does not explain that effect.
When a judgment stops at “clear” or “smoother,” read the [contrasting readings and revisions](reading-examples.md) before choosing edits.

Then follow the reader's likely paths: skimming headings, searching for an answer, skipping familiar sections, or reading an argument continuously.
Can they recognize where to go and understand what they find without the originating session?
Do headings promise what their sections deliver?
A reference may need separate lookup destinations where an argument needs a connected paragraph.

### Check what the evidence supports

Compare claims with the supplied inputs; inspect additional sources for unsupported claims or contradictions.
Distinguish observations, decisions, proposals, examples, hypotheses, and unresolved questions.
Preserve the author's goals and experience: “I want to use this” is not “I use this.”
Attribute checks to whoever performed them.
Your inability to run a test does not establish that the author never ran it; do not invent first-person disclaimers.
Describe the prose's effects, not the author's identity or effort.

## 2. Develop the explanation

Make the title and opening intelligible without session context.
What needs explaining before this reader can follow the next passage?
Define unfamiliar terms before consequential use, but keep deliberate concrete-to-general development.
Needing a concept later does not mean its name must precede the example that teaches it.

### Make choices and requests understandable

For a proposal, name the requested change before explaining workarounds unless the required form dictates otherwise.
Read the title and opening together: do they ask for the same thing?
Do not turn a change request into general advice-seeking, require a fix proposal for a support question, or ask someone to design an alternative the author did not request.
See the [request-before-workaround contrast](reading-examples.md#state-the-request-before-the-workaround).

When writing supports a choice, explain what each relevant option changes for this reader and when that difference matters.
An inventory of settings does not explain their consequences; a recommendation without reasons does not either.
What connection must the reader supply, and is that a reasonable inference from their background and the text?
Make the judgment rather than leaving the reader to discover every consequence alone.
Stay within the evidence: a missing explanation is not proof of a product defect.

For a distinction, what changes in the reader's understanding or choice?
Remove or rework distinctions that change neither.
For a corrective contrast, what plausible misconception would it correct?
Do not invent confusion to justify a lesson.

### Show why one step leads to the next

For a nontrivial mechanism, explain the problem, show a concrete example or trace, then explain the solution and its invariant.
Explain the property that makes a detail relevant, not just its name.
For a loop, walk through one iteration: how does its end leave the condition for another?
Numbered steps, source links, and “therefore” cannot supply a missing connection.
Compare the [property-and-consequence and feedback-loop examples](reading-examples.md#explain-what-the-detail-changes-for-the-reader) when facts remain disconnected.

Develop a hypothesis's proposed mechanism without claiming confirmation.
Keep observations distinguishable from the diagnosis so they remain useful if it changes.
If an essential connection lacks evidence, identify the gap rather than inventing support or retreating to disconnected facts.
Explain why a schema or invariant exists before listing its fields.
Introduce conceptual layers in an order the reader can follow, not necessarily the source's sentence order.

### Give detail the right space

Give a thought room to develop before polishing its sentences.
Joining sentences can retain every fact while burying their relationship.
Words can earn attention through rhythm, emphasis, humor, or a changed feeling as well as information.

For decision-oriented writing, keep the problem, stakes, requested outcome, decisive evidence, and material limits of alternatives in the main text.
Move supporting traces, setup, and background to appendices or collapsed details when the format allows it; link instead of repeating them.
Do not force essays, references, or instructions into a proposal's progression.

For important measurements, state what was counted, the population or denominator, input revision or time range, method, and limitations.
Explain what the number demonstrates and whether it is provisional or an acceptance constant.
Compare comparable results, not just a winning conclusion.
Use labeled tables or definition lists; use bullets or short subsections for commands, long prose, or more than three columns.

## 3. Revise, then cut

Fix the reading problem, not just its sentences.
A buried relationship may need reordering; an instruction list may need [concrete questions and examples](reading-examples.md#turn-abstract-instructions-into-concrete-questions).
Build each paragraph around a developing thought, contrast, or effect rather than a shared broad topic.
Preserve effective voice, deliberate casing, fragments, idiom, and precise terms.
Voice also lives in what the author notices and connects, not only verbal personality.
Apply vocabulary bans by meaning, not word matching.

Prefer familiar words and active verbs with concrete subjects: “the launcher calls Python,” not “Python is called.”
Name actors when responsibility or evidence matters, without inventing one to eliminate passive voice.
Distinguish a deliberately skipped check from one blocked by missing access or inputs.
Give the reason when it changes the conclusion or next action.
Limit claims about unperformed checks to the investigation whose records you have.

Make an aggressive deletion pass once the explanation works.
Edit like you are a chainsaw-wielding maniac that just discovered the sentences are young adults who came to the lake at summer camp after sunset.
Cut repeated claims, investigation narration, defensive qualifications, announcements, and paraphrases with no further purpose.
Keep the request, concrete motivation, decisive evidence, and qualifications that change the conclusion or next action.
Keep rationale, rejected alternatives, and examples that guide a future choice or prevent a likely mistake.
Restore anything whose loss makes the reader reconstruct a necessary connection.

Preserve requirements, authority, priority, defaults, conditions, exceptions, commands, paths, and validation.
Moving an unconditional rule under “When tests fail” makes it conditional even if its words stay the same.
Keep dependent conditions together and warnings beside their actions.
Keep rationale and examples distinguishable from rules; example-only behavior must not become a default or guarantee.
When removing duplicated policy, use the [consolidation check](consolidation.md).
For skills, preserve discovery triggers and check references and examples.

## 4. Compare the complete result

Read the complete result as prose before defending individual edits, then compare it with the original.
Restore anything that reads better there.
Skim headings and read changed sections independently: can readers still find their actions and constraints?
Check terms, numbers, examples, and single or composed cases for consistency.
For instructions, can the reader act without inventing policy?

Support the judgment with representative passages and sustained patterns, not an edit tally.
Apply the same standard to writing and reviewing: recoverable meaning does not make prose worth following.
Name remaining reading problems even when the revision improves the whole.

Leaving the artifact unchanged needs comparison with the strongest concrete revision considered, not just a defense of its facts.
If the verdict is “all the information is necessary,” read the [lookup contrast](reading-examples.md#needed-facts-can-still-be-hard-to-find).
If evidence is still insufficient, obtain a bounded independent whole-artifact reading when available or report the limitation.
Reconsider missed improvements within this pass rather than starting the workflow again.

## 5. Validate and deliver

For edits, check the final form readers consume: render or compile when relevant tooling exists, check changed links and examples, and run applicable repository checks.
Do not edit generated output directly.
Required repository checks must pass; report failed or unavailable checks and unread or excluded scope.
Distinguish the reading judgment from operational behavior actually verified.
Do not imply you inspected rendering when you did not.

Finish with a supported whole-artifact judgment, consequential preservation choices, and unresolved reading problems.
The reader must be able to understand and use the result without session context; consequential claims need an authority or stated uncertainty.
For review-only work, report prioritized findings with evidence, consequences, and the smallest useful corrections.
Use `tighten-prose` only for a remaining local problem, not as a mandatory second pass.
